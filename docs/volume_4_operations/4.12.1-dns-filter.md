## 4.12.1.1 DNS filter (AdGuard Home, on by default)

Setup turns it on unless you say otherwise (`dns_filter: none`, or *Advanced* on setup's plan; design [dns-filter.md](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.1-adguard.md#11211-status)): AdGuard Home answers the
network's DNS on `host_ip:53` and BIND moves to port 5053 (`bind_dns_port`), where RFC2136 clients and
linked federation sites find it. DHCP hands out the host's address as before.

Out of the box AdGuard sends internet lookups to **Cloudflare** over DNS-over-HTTPS and blocks with the
**AdGuard DNS filter** list; fabric's names always go to this site's BIND and are never blocked. Setup's plan
lets you type other upstreams in *Advanced*. To change it later:

1. Open `https://adguard.<domain>` and sign in (Keycloak, TOTP). You need the `dns:filter` permission
   (admins and network operators have it).
2. In AdGuard: **Settings → DNS settings** — your upstreams and bootstrap servers; **Filters** — the block
   lists you want; **Custom filtering rules** — your own rules go below fabric's marked section.

With Keycloak off (setup's Advanced), AdGuard's page cannot be signed in to; DNS still works.

fabric keeps everything you set there across deploys. It manages only its own part: the
`[/<zone>/]10.255.0.30` lines that send fabric's zones (this site's, the organisation's, linked sites',
reverse zones) to BIND, the allow rules that keep those names from ever being blocked, the LAN-only
client list, AdGuard's ports, its local login (sent by nginx after your sign-in) and DHCP off. Your own
AdGuard elsewhere instead: keep `dns_filter: none` and point it at the site's BIND on `bind_dns_port`.

Two units: `adguard` (the DNS filter) and `adguard-auth` (the sign-in in front of its UI). They start and
stop apart: with Keycloak or the sign-in down only `https://adguard.<domain>` is unreachable, DNS keeps
answering, and a BIND restart (every DNS apply) does not touch AdGuard.

## 4.12.1.2 The BIND resolver (0.7)

Being built for 0.7 (design [1.12.2](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.12.2-bind-resolver.md#11221-status));
it replaces AdGuard Home when 0.7 is released. Until then it is chosen with `dns_filter: bind` in the vars file:
a second BIND answers the network on `host_ip:53`, BIND moves to 5053 as with AdGuard, and fabric's names are never
filtered.

- **Lists, rules and upstreams** are settings ([1.1.9.9](../volume_1_description_and_architecture/1.1.9-optional-parts.md#1199-dns-filter-the-bind-resolver-07)):
  `dns_filter_lists` (`{name, url}`; the AdGuard DNS filter by default), `dns_filter_allow` and `dns_filter_block`
  (each name with every name below it), `dns_filter_upstreams` (Cloudflare over DNS-over-TLS by default). Change
  them, then apply (`sudo fabricctl --apply`): the running resolver reloads, nothing restarts.
- **`sudo fabricctl dns-filter status`**: whether the resolver answers, and for each list when it was fetched, its
  last good copy, the rules converted and skipped (rules a response policy zone cannot express) and the memory it
  takes. Exit 1 while a list has no good copy.
- **`sudo fabricctl dns-filter lists`**: fetch every list now; only the lists that changed are reloaded. The
  `fabric-dns-lists` timer does the same every night; a list that cannot be fetched keeps its last good copy.
- **Why was a name blocked?** `<base>/resolver/log/rpz.log` names the client, the name and the zone that matched:
  `owner.rpz` (your rules) or `<id>.list.rpz`, one of the lists (`<id>` comes from its URL: 1.12.2.6; the web console will name the
  list, step 5 of 0.7).
- Memory: the default list takes about 190 MiB of the resolver's 384 MiB (`resolver_mem_limit`); the status shows
  what each list takes.
