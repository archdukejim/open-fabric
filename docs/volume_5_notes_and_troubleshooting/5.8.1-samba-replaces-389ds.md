## 5.8.1.1 Status and what the owner decided

Status: **direction approved by the owner on 2026-10-04, gated by the S0 spike** (5.8.1.5). Nothing is rebuilt
until the spike has answered every question in 5.8.1.5 and the owner has approved the rewritten designs (global
Rules 4 and 5.5). It changes the Samba design (2.11.1), which kept 389-DS as the source of truth beside an
optional Samba.

The owner's answers to the Samba decisions (1.2.6), 2026-10-04:

- **S1 (D73), where passwords and people live:** "If we can replace 389-DS with Samba I think we should and simplify
  the container stack." Samba AD becomes fabric's one directory; 389-DS goes.
- **S2 (D74), site controllers:** the owner asked for "federated levels": a site's admins change only their site,
  while the configuration replicates everywhere. AD's answer within one domain: an organisational unit (OU) per
  site with rights delegated to that site's admins; schema, configuration and Group Policy replicate to every
  controller; read-only controllers (RODCs) where the spike proves them. A forest of several domains (true
  per-site domains) is, as far as is known, not supported by Samba; the spike confirms it.
- **S3 (D75), the controller's address:** the host's address. Ports 389/636 are free once 389-DS is gone; BIND
  keeps 53 and serves the AD zone through Samba's DLZ plugin.
- **S4 (D76), Linux joins:** follows S1 (there is only AD to join); the owner confirms it with the rewritten
  design.
- S5 (D77) and S6 (D78): not yet answered.

## 5.8.1.2 What changes

Today 389-DS is fabric's directory: people, groups, POSIX ids, devices and their roles, the address plan, split
into an organisation part and site parts and replicated between sites (M1, M1b, M5). Samba AD would take all of
that over and add what only AD gives: Windows logons and Group Policy, Kerberos single sign-on for Linux,
PEAP-MSCHAPv2 for 802.1X, one password store. The stack loses a container (`dirsrv`) and a replication system
(fabric's own agreements and `fabric-directory-sync`), and the hardest part of the old Samba design — keeping
passwords equal in two directories — disappears.

## 5.8.1.3 What replaces what

| Today (389-DS) | With Samba AD |
|---|---|
| `dirsrv` container, `ldap_base_dn`, organisation suffix + `ou=<site>` sub-suffixes (M1, M1b) | `samba` container (a DC), realm `AD.<DOMAIN>`; the organisation's objects in the domain, one OU per site |
| M5 replication: agreements, read-only organisation at sites, `fabric-directory-sync.timer` | AD replication between the site DCs; AD sites built from the address plan; RODCs at sites if proven |
| fabric's schema (`05-schema.ldif`: 15 attributes and classes for devices, device roles, networks, sites) | The same attributes added to AD's schema (permanent once added) or kept as fabric data elsewhere — decided by the spike |
| ACIs and fabric's service accounts (`30-aci.ldif`, `20-accounts.ldif`) | AD ACLs on OUs (`samba-tool dsacl`), delegated per site; service accounts as AD users |
| Keycloak federates to 389-DS (writable) | Keycloak federates to AD (its LDAP provider in AD mode; passwords checked by AD) |
| FreeRADIUS looks devices and people up in 389-DS; EAP-TTLS checks passwords by LDAP bind | FreeRADIUS looks up in AD; PEAP-MSCHAPv2 through `ntlm_auth` (winbind); EAP-TLS unchanged |
| Linux clients: SSSD against 389-DS (M10 steps 1–3) | Linux clients join AD (`realm join`, Kerberos); sudo rules in AD (the sudo schema) |
| POSIX ids from fabric (`ensure_posix_identities`) | RFC 2307 attributes in AD (`--use-rfc2307`), assigned by fabric as today |
| The web UI's directory pages and fabric-agent through `fabriclib/ldap` | The same pages and routes through a directory layer that speaks to AD |
| Address plan stored in the directory (M8) | In AD (schema) or in fabric's own data, replicated with it — decided by the spike |
| BIND only; AD DNS behind it (old design, macvlan) | BIND serves fabric's zones and, through DLZ, the AD zone; Windows registers its names by signed dynamic updates |

## 5.8.1.4 Impact assessment

**Architecture.** The directory moves from 389-DS to Samba AD everywhere. 1.3 (topology), 1.5 (agent API), 1.8
(federation: M1, M1b, M5), 2.2.2.6 (address plan), 2.10.1 (Linux join), 2.11.1 (Samba) and the container
inventory (1.3.8, D49) are rewritten. Keycloak, FreeRADIUS, BIND, Step-CA and OpenBao stay; their links to the
directory change. The core-services rule (1.9.1: DNS, DHCP, LDAP, SSO and nginx never depend on OpenBao to start)
applies to the DC as it did to 389-DS.

**Breaking changes.**

- Every existing install migrates once: people, groups, POSIX ids, devices, device roles and the address plan
  move from 389-DS to AD; **passwords cannot move** (389-DS keeps only one-way hashes), so every person sets a new
  password at the first sign-in after the upgrade (Keycloak's required action). Installs are few (the owner's
  two houses and test machines), so a guided migration plus that one reset is proposed.
- Federation: sites join the AD domain instead of a 389-DS replica; the invitation carries what a DC needs to join.
  Existing sites re-join.
- The fabric-agent routes for people, devices and roles keep their shape; their backend changes.
- The memory budget at 4 GB changes (a DC instead of 389-DS; measured in the spike).
- The host publishes the DC's ports (88, 135, 389, 445, 464, 636, 3268/3269, the RPC range): a host change that
  goes through consent (2.7.1).

**Scope creep risk: high.** It touches most of fabric. Contained by: the spike first (5.8.1.5) — if a question
fails, the replacement stops and the old design (Samba beside 389-DS) is the fallback; then one rewritten design
approved before any code; then the build in steps, each tested, with 389-DS kept running until the AD side passes
the same suites (dirsrv, keycloak, freeradius, federation, sandbox).

## 5.8.1.5 The S0 spike: the questions it must answer

A throwaway build in `spikes/samba/` (Rule 4: never merged into `src/`), real containers, on Ubuntu's own Samba
packages. Each question has a pass condition; any failure is reported to the owner before anything else.

| # | Question | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | A DC in a hardened container on the **host's address** (S3) | Provisions idempotently; runs with only the capabilities it needs, no-new-privileges, a memory limit; a re-run converges |
| Q2 | **BIND with DLZ** in fabric's BIND image | BIND answers the AD zone from Samba's database; a Linux member's `net ads dns register` (signed update) lands; fabric's own zones unchanged |
| Q3 | **fabric's data in AD**: devices, device roles, networks | Either a schema extension works (and survives a second DC) or the data is kept as fabric data; the spike recommends one |
| Q4 | **Per-site delegation** (S2) | A site admin can create, change and remove objects in its OU and is refused everywhere else |
| Q5 | **Several domains in one forest** | Confirmed unsupported (or supported) on the current Samba; recorded |
| Q6 | **RODC** at a site (S2) | Joins, answers logons and lookups with the root DC stopped; refuses writes |
| Q7 | **Keycloak against AD** | Users from AD sign in with TOTP; a new password set in Keycloak works on AD logons |
| Q8 | **FreeRADIUS against AD** | PEAP-MSCHAPv2 for a person and for a machine account; EAP-TLS unchanged; a disabled account refused |
| Q9 | **Linux join** (S4) | `realm join`, `kinit`, an SSH login, sudo from AD, refusal outside the allowed groups |
| Q10 | **Memory at 4 GB** | The stack with the DC fits the 4 GB budget (1.3.8) with its limits set |
| Q11 | **Migration** | A 389-DS directory from today's seed is moved into AD by a script: every person, group, id, device and role arrives |

## 5.8.1.6 After the spike

The spike's results are recorded here (a new topic per run). The owner then approves or rejects the replacement on
them; on approval, the designs listed in 5.8.1.4 are rewritten (each change with its own impact noted) and the build

## 5.8.1.7 Spike results, run 1 (2026-10-04)

`spikes/samba/` (README there), WSL amd64, Debian trixie's Samba **4.22.11** and BIND **9.20.29**. A first finding
changes the old design: the DC is built from **Debian's** packages on fabric's pinned Debian base, not Ubuntu's —
Samba's DLZ module must match BIND's version, and fabric's BIND is Debian's (Ubuntu 24.04 has Samba 4.19 and
BIND 9.18). Samba documents `dlz_bind9_18` as the module for BIND 9.18 and 9.20.

| # | Result | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **Pass** | Provisions on a read-only root with every capability dropped but CHOWN, DAC_OVERRIDE, FOWNER, SETUID, SETGID; afterwards it runs with **SETGID and SETUID** (SETUID for ordinary users' share access, run 2; Docker already allows binding low ports in the container); NET_BIND_SERVICE not needed. The administrator's password is set from a file through Samba's Python bindings (no argv); provisioning prints its temporary random password to the log, so the real image must discard that output. Runtime sockets need `/run` (`ntp signd socket directory`), core dumps `/var/log/samba` (tmpfs). About 240 MiB used of a 512 MiB limit. A re-run on the provisioned data converges. The DC must have its **full host name** (`dc1.ad.lan.test`), or Kerberos asks for the wrong service name. The two early exits (code 255) were not Samba: the WSL distribution idled out between test commands and Docker restarted with it (journal times match; run 2) |
| Q2 | **Pass** | fabric's BIND (uid 600, no capabilities, read-only root) loads `dlz_bind9_18.so` with Samba's `samba-libs` and `samba-dsdb-modules` added to its image; it needs the DC's `smb.conf` (read-only) and its `bind-dns` folder, `--url` naming the database, and a writable `/var/tmp` for Kerberos' replay cache. It answers the AD zone and fabric's own zone unchanged; the DC's signed (GSS-TSIG) updates are accepted; unsigned updates are refused in both zones. The DC image needs a `bind` group with fabric's gid (600) so provisioning grants BIND access |
| Q4 | **Pass** | An OU per site under `OU=sites`; one ACL (`samba-tool dsacl`, container-inherited) gives a site's admin group full control below its OU: a site admin adds and removes objects there and is refused in another site, at the domain level and on the domain's administrator |
| Q5 | **Confirmed unsupported** | `samba-tool domain join` offers MEMBER, DC and RODC only: no child domains. Sites are OUs in one domain (S2 as answered) |
| Q3, Q6–Q11 | See run 2 (Q3, Q6) | |

## 5.8.1.8 Spike results, run 2 (2026-10-04)

Q1–Q6 re-run from scratch with `q1-q2.sh`, `q4.sh` and `q3-q6.sh`: every check passes.

| # | Result | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 | **Pass: a schema extension** | fabric's 11 attributes and 4 classes load into AD's schema and the directory checks clean (`samba-tool dbcheck`). **fabric's OIDs must change**: AD refuses an OID component above 32 bits, so the UUID arc `2.25.<UUID>` is invalid there; the same UUID under Microsoft's arc for unregistered extensions (`1.2.840.113556.1.8000.2554.<GUID in seven parts>`) works. The schema is loaded in two sessions (attributes, then the classes that use them). A site admin creates a device (`device` + `ieee802Device` + fabric's classes, the MAC in `macAddress`) and a network of the address plan in its OU; fabric's attributes are searchable; a network without its CIDR is refused. **Recommendation: the extension**, not fabric data kept elsewhere: one store, replicated and access-controlled by AD |
| Q6 | **Pass, with one limit** | An RODC joins (hardened like the DC; about 190 MiB) and gets the domain, the extended schema and the site's devices. With the root DC up, its KDC forwards Kerberos for accounts it does not cache. With the root DC stopped: a site user whose password is cached (the *Allowed RODC Password Replication Group*, preloaded) signs in by Kerberos and NTLM; the administrator (never cached) is refused; writes are refused. **Limit:** NTLM sign-ins for accounts the RODC does not cache are **not** forwarded to the root DC even when it is up. PEAP-MSCHAPv2 (802.1X) is NTLM, so at a site with an RODC a person's password must be cached there, or FreeRADIUS must check NTLM against a writable DC — to settle in Q8 |
| Q1 (rerun) | Pass | The two early "exits" of run 1 were the WSL distribution idling out (Docker restarted each time); not Samba |

| Q7 | **Pass** | Keycloak 26.7.4 (the pinned image) with an LDAP provider in AD mode, writable, over LDAPS trusting the DC's CA (its bind password from a file): an AD user signs in through Keycloak; a wrong password and a user disabled in AD are refused; a password reset in Keycloak lands in AD (SMB sign-in with it works). **Finding:** AD accepts the previous password for network sign-ins for 60 minutes after a change (`old password allowed period`); fabric decides whether to set it to 0. TOTP not re-tested: it is Keycloak's own data, unchanged by the user store (the `keycloak` suite covers it) |
| Q1 (rerun) | Pass | **SETUID is needed at runtime too**: without it ordinary users cannot read shares (SMB as a non-admin refused), which SYSVOL and Group Policy need. Runtime capabilities: SETGID and SETUID |

| Q8 | **Pass** | FreeRADIUS (Debian's, uid 610, no capabilities, read-only root, about 45 MiB) checks PEAP-MSCHAPv2 through `ntlm_auth` and the DC's own winbind — no separate member join: the DC's winbind sockets are shared into the FreeRADIUS container. A person and a machine (`host/ws1.<domain>` rewritten to `ws1$`) are accepted; a wrong password and a disabled account are refused. Needed: `ntlm auth = mschapv2-and-ntlmv2-only` in the DC's `smb.conf` (Samba refuses MS-CHAPv2 by default; `winbindd` reads only `smb.conf`, not the master's `--option`); winbind's privileged folder in group 610 (Samba checks only its owner and mode, so fabric's deploy sets the group); the container needs its `ssl-cert` group added explicitly (`--user` drops supplementary groups). EAP-TLS is unchanged (no directory password). **Design point from Q6:** at a site with an RODC, PEAP for a person works when that person's password is cached at the site (its *Allowed RODC Password Replication Group* = the site's people), or FreeRADIUS uses a writable DC's winbind |

| Q9 | **Pass** | An Ubuntu 24.04 machine joins with `adcli` (password on stdin) into **its site's OU**; SSSD (AD provider, `ldap_id_mapping = false`) gives identities with fabric's own POSIX ids and groups from AD; `kinit` works on the machine; login allowed for the permitted group (`simple_allow_groups`) and refused for others; sudo rules come from AD. Ubuntu no longer ships sudo's AD schema, so it is defined with sudo's registered OIDs (`1.3.6.1.4.1.15953.9`, which fit AD). **Finding:** a structural class added to AD's schema needs a `defaultSecurityDescriptor` granting authenticated users read, or its objects are visible to admins only (the sudo rules were invisible to the machine until then); fabric's `fabricNetwork` gets the same. Not tested here: an SSH password login (the PAM account check and Kerberos stand in; S6 tests it) |
| Q6 (follow-up) | Corrected | In run 2 the RODC did not forward NTLM for an account it does not cache; in three later runs it did, after both DCs' `smb.conf` gained `ntlm auth = mschapv2-and-ntlmv2-only` (Q8). Likely the cause, not proven: S1 tests NTLM forwarding at an RODC explicitly before the PEAP-at-sites design relies on it |

| Q10 | **Pass (light load, amd64)** | Peak memory (cgroup `memory.peak`) after the spike's work: DC 229 MiB, RODC 254 MiB, BIND with DLZ 69 MiB, FreeRADIUS 77 MiB, Keycloak 1124 MiB (dev mode). Proposed limits at 4 GB: the DC 512 MiB (389-DS has 384), BIND 128 MiB (80 today: too tight with Samba's DLZ libraries loaded); FreeRADIUS and Keycloak unchanged. With 389-DS gone the budget grows by about 176 MiB on today's ≈ 2.3 GB: it fits 4 GB. Not measured: real load, and the Pi (arm64) itself — S1 measures there |
| Q11 | **Pass** | A real 389-DS from fabric's image and rendered seed, filled the way fabric fills it (device roles and devices through `fabriclib.ldap`, people with POSIX ids, a site network), exported and written into AD by `q11-migrate.py`: every person (with ids), group (with gid and members), device role (VLAN, permissions), device (MACs, roles, certificate fingerprints) and network arrived — 16 entries compared one by one. Mapping: people to `OU=people`, groups to `OU=groups`, device roles to AD groups with the `fabricRole` class in `OU=device-roles`, devices and networks to the site's OU; fabric's everyone-group `users` becomes AD's **Domain Users** with fabric's gid (5000), because AD names must be unique across the domain. Not carried over, by design: 389-DS's seven service accounts (AD has its own, with delegated rights) and every **password** (one-way hashes): migrated people are **disabled until they set a password** |

Run 3 (2026-10-04): Q1–Q9 re-run from a fresh domain, every check passes; Q10 measured, Q11 run on a fresh domain.

## 5.8.1.9 Outcome of the spike

**Every question passes.** Samba AD can replace 389-DS as fabric's directory, built from Debian's packages on
fabric's base, hardened like the other containers. What the rewritten design must carry (each from a result above):

- The DC runs with SETGID and SETUID only (provisioning and joining need CHOWN, DAC_OVERRIDE and FOWNER too),
  read-only, its full host name, its sockets on `/run`; provisioning output discarded (it prints a temporary
  password); the administrator's password set from a file.
- BIND gains Samba's DLZ module (`samba-libs`, `samba-dsdb-modules`), the DC's `smb.conf` read-only, the DC's
  `bind-dns` folder and a writable `/var/tmp`; its limit goes to 128 MiB.
- fabric's schema moves to an AD-valid OID arc derived from its UUID, loaded in two sessions; structural classes
  carry a default security descriptor.
- Sites are OUs in one domain with delegated rights (Samba has no child domains); RODCs work (Kerberos forwarded,
  cached sign-ins with the root DC down, writes refused); NTLM forwarding at an RODC is tested again in S1.
- Keycloak federates to AD (writable, LDAPS); fabric decides the old-password window (60 minutes by default).
- FreeRADIUS uses the DC's winbind for PEAP (`ntlm auth = mschapv2-and-ntlmv2-only` in `smb.conf`, the
  privileged folder in its group); EAP-TLS unchanged.
- Linux machines join with adcli into their site's OU; SSSD gives fabric's ids, access by group and sudo from AD
  (sudo's schema with its own OIDs).
- Migration moves everything but passwords and 389-DS's service accounts; people set a new password once.

The owner now approves or rejects the replacement (5.8.1.6); on approval the designs of 5.8.1.4 are rewritten.
