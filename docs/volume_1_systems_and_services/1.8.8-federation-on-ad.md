## 1.8.8.2 What changes and what stays

| Stays (1.8.1–1.8.5) | Changes |
|---|---|
| Sites, the root site, attachment flat / relay / nested, invitations, the federation endpoint | Directory: one AD domain; each site an OU and, by choice, a DC |
| Each site's intermediate CA, signed by the root (or its parent) | M5's replication (agreements, changelog, `fabric-directory-sync`) gives way to AD's own replication |
| DNS: delegated sub-zones, secondaries both ways with TSIG (M4) | The AD zone is served by each site's BIND through DLZ where the site has a DC |

## 1.8.8.3 A site's DC: writable, read-only or none

Chosen at the invitation (`fabricctl federation invite lab --dc writable|rodc|none`), changeable later (1.8.8.12).
Every site runs the full stack whatever it chooses (D91). The root site always has a writable DC; it holds the
domain-wide roles (FSMO: schema, naming, RID, PDC emulator, infrastructure).

| | Writable | Read-only (RODC) | None |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holds | the whole domain, every password hash | the whole domain read-only; passwords only of the site's *Allowed RODC Password Replication Group* (the site's people and machines), preloaded | nothing: the site's services use a DC of its parent |
| Writes at the site | its own OU (and whatever overrides allow), offline too | none; writes go to a writable DC over the link (refused while cut off) | none locally; over the link |
| Sign-ins while cut off | everyone it may admit | cached people and machines; others refused | Windows' and SSSD's cached sign-ins on the machines only |
| If the host is taken | the domain is | that site's cached passwords are | nothing beyond the site's own services |

- Setup and the Federation tab state the last row next to the choice.
- NTLM forwarding at an RODC for uncached accounts (Q6 follow-up) was confirmed in S1 (2026-10-04, `tests/samba`):
  with the writable DC reachable, PEAP at an RODC site works for everyone; with it cut off, for the site's cached
  people and machines only.

## 1.8.8.4 Joining a site

The join of 1.8.4.1 is kept: invitation (fingerprint, one-time secret), the endpoint over TLS, the intermediate CA,
the TSIG key and delegation. The answer adds:

- the AD domain, realm and NetBIOS name, and the root DC's address;
- the site's OU, groups (`<site>-users`, `<site>-admins`), service accounts, ACLs and id block, **created by the
  parent before it answers** (by the parent's agent through its nearest writable DC, which its inherited rights allow, D105);
- for a writable or read-only DC: a **temporary join account** with a random password (in the TLS answer, never on
  a command line), valid for this join only and deleted when the join is done or after one hour. Samba's DC join
  needs domain-admin rights; S8 establishes whether less is enough, and uses the least that works;
- the site's service-account passwords, which go straight into the site's OpenBao.

Setup then joins the DC (`samba-tool domain join … DC|RODC`, into the AD site of the same name), registers its
names in AD's DNS (replication finds partners by them, Q12), preloads an RODC's cache, and starts the rest of the
stack against it. A site with no DC skips the DC and points its services at its parent's DC.

## 1.8.8.5 Replication between DCs

- AD's own replication (DRS) between DCs; its topology is AD's (the KCC) from **AD sites and site links**, which
  fabric writes: one AD site per fabric site, its subnets from the address plan (2.2.2.8), one site link from each
  site to its parent (cost 100, every 15 minutes). Windows machines find their own site's DC by their subnet.
- **Ports between DCs:** 53, 88, 135, 389, 445, 464, 636, 3268, 3269 and the DC's RPC range (2.11.2.4) between
  the two hosts' addresses; the federation link opens them to the peer's address only, as for DNS today (1.8.4.2).
- Conflicts follow AD's rules (Q12): the later write of an attribute wins; a name created on both sides keeps
  both objects, one renamed `CNF:`. fabric's doctor lists `CNF:` objects for an admin to resolve.
- A site cut off longer than AD's tombstone lifetime (180 days) must be re-joined; doctor warns well before.

## 1.8.8.7 Offline behaviour

1.8.3.3 holds with these differences: a site with a writable DC keeps creating and changing its own people,
groups, machines and devices while cut off (they replicate when the link returns); a read-only site is as today
(changes wait for the link); a site with no DC loses domain sign-ins except what machines cache, and fabric's
status says so whenever its DC is unreachable. Every site keeps DNS, DHCP, its CA, its secrets and EAP-TLS.

## 1.8.8.9 The Federation tab: limits, overrides and grants

Built with M6:

- **Sites:** name, parent, relay, DC type, link and replication health, last replication, `CNF:` objects.
- **Limits:** each site's default write limits (1.6.3.6), as a table; **overrides** added and removed by the root's
  admins (`federation:admin`), each with who and when.
- **Access grants** (1.6.3.10): explicit and blanket, per site.
- **Address plan:** site → network → VLAN → notes (2.2.2.8).
- Overrides and grants are kept in fabric's database, a copy at every site ([1.8.8.16](#18816-where-overrides-and-grants-are-kept)).

## 1.8.8.10 Sign-in and 802.1X at each type of site

- **Keycloak** at each site federates its own DC (or, with none, its parent's), writable. At an RODC site writes
  come back as referrals to a writable DC; Keycloak follows them (to be proven in S8; if it cannot, Keycloak there
  is configured with the writable DC for writes and the RODC for reads).
- **FreeRADIUS** uses its own site's DC's winbind; at a site with no DC, PEAP needs the link (EAP-TLS and MAB keep
  working from the site's own data, read from the parent's DC and cached).

## 1.8.8.11 HA partners

Phase F3's "389-DS multi-supplier" becomes **two writable DCs in one site** (AD multi-master; each node's services
use their local DC). The rest of 1.8.2 is unchanged.

## 1.8.8.12 Removing a site, changing its DC type

- **Changing the type** (`fabricctl federation dc-type <site> writable|rodc|none`): demote, then join again under
  the same name (Q15); the site's services use the parent's DC meanwhile; nothing else of the site changes.
- **Removing a site** (`fabricctl federation remove <site>`, by its parent): its DC is removed from the domain
  (`samba-tool domain demote --remove-other-dead-server` when it is gone), its AD site, site link, service
  accounts and grants go; its OU (people, machines, devices) is moved to the parent's or deleted, as the admin
  chooses; its CA is revoked (F7).

## 1.8.8.14 Nesting and inheritance

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (D105); replaces 1.8.8.6.

- **A site's OU is in its parent's OU.** The root site's OU is the only one directly under `OU=sites`; a site that
  joins the root gets `OU=<site>,OU=<root>,OU=sites`, one nested under it `OU=<child>,OU=<site>,OU=<root>,OU=sites`.
  The parent is also recorded on the OU (`fabricParentSite`). Nesting in the directory follows the attachment of
  1.8.5.1; the CA's path length is unchanged.
- **Inherited by default, from the domain down:**
  - Group Policy: AD's own GPO inheritance. A GPO linked to the domain or to a parent's OU applies to every site
    below it. A child overrides a setting with its own GPO (the closer link wins) or blocks inheritance on its OU
    (`<site>-gpo-admins`, D103).
  - **Controls:** a parent links a GPO **enforced** when its children may not change it. An enforced link wins
    over the child's own GPOs and passes a blocked inheritance; only the parent's GPO admins set or clear it.
  - Permissions: AD's ACL inheritance. A parent's agent and roles reach every site below it; a child cannot remove
    an inherited ACE.
- **A site with no DC** uses its nearest ancestor that has one, writable for writes (the parent's, else the next
  one up): its services read and write there over LDAPS with the site's own service accounts, whose ACLs are
  limited to its OU (1.6.3.6). While that DC is unreachable it keeps what 1.8.8.7 says.
- **A nested parent prepares its child** with its own agent through its nearest writable DC (its inherited rights
  cover the child's OU); the root is asked only when the parent has no writable DC on its path.
- **Re-parenting moves the OU** (an LDAP rename of the subtree), with its CA and site link as today. Objects keep
  their SIDs and GUIDs, so GPO links and group memberships hold; anything fabric keeps by DN is found again by the
  site's name (joined Linux machines search sudo rules by a filter, not under a DN).
- **Relays forward joins and status only.** DC replication goes directly between the site's DC and its parent's
  over routed connectivity (D92); a relay node is an ordinary site.

## 1.8.8.15 Group Policy between sites over SMB

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (D94 revised); replaces 1.8.8.8.

- **Each GPO has an owning site:** the site whose OU it is linked to; the root for domain-linked GPOs. Only the
  owner's agent (or its GPO admins with RSAT at the owner's DC) writes its folder.
- **Every DC pulls the GPO folders it does not own** from the owner's DC over SMB (`smbclient` in the DC's
  container, Kerberos as the DC's own machine account; 445 is open between DCs, 1.8.8.5), every 5 minutes. A copy
  is taken only when the GPO object's version (which AD replicates) is newer than the local folder's `GPT.INI`, and
  only once the owner's `GPT.INI` matches the object: object and files that disagree are reported, never applied
  half. Each folder's ACLs are then set from its object, as for a GPO fabric writes itself.
- An RODC pulls everything. A GPO changed at a DC that does not own it is overwritten by the owner's copy, and
  doctor says so.
- **SYSVOL and NETLOGON are never advertised** (the owner's condition): both shares are `browseable = no`, so they
  are not listed when `\\<dc>` is opened; NetBIOS is off (`disable netbios`, nothing on 137–139); no mDNS
  (`multicast dns register = no`) and no WS-Discovery service. Windows reads policy by its path
  (`\\<domain>\SYSVOL\...`), which hiding does not change.

## 1.8.8.16 Where overrides and grants are kept

Decided by the owner 2026-10-05 (D104).

- **fabric's database:** a `fabric` database and role in each site's Postgres (the server Keycloak uses); its
  password in the site's OpenBao. One table each for overrides and grants (who, when, what, the owning site).
- **One writer per row:** a row belongs to the site that made it (a parent's override on a child is the parent's);
  only that site writes it.
- **A copy everywhere:** Postgres's logical replication along the site tree; each site publishes to its parent and
  its children the rows they do not own, so every site holds every row and nothing loops. A site that loses its
  database gets every row back from its parent when it is reinstalled or rejoined.
- **Postgres is never on the host.** A link reaches the peer's Postgres through nginx's `stream` proxy: one port,
  open in the firewall to the linked sites' addresses only; TLS that requires a client certificate signed by the
  organisation's root, whose name nginx checks against the linked sites; then TLS again from nginx to Postgres
  (D51), and a replication role per link that may only replicate these tables.
- **The effects are in AD**, as before (ACLs for overrides; GPO, FreeRADIUS and Keycloak changes for grants), so
  losing the table never takes anyone's access away by itself.
